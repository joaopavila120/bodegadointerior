## tourism-landmarks

Production pixel art sprite atlas for an elevated orthographic top-down rural southern Brazil RPG. Exactly SIX fully separate landmark vignettes, 3 columns by 2 rows, equal 512x512 cells in a 1536x1024 canvas. Each sprite fits within central 410x400 pixels of its cell, generous transparent gutters and padding, no content crosses cell boundaries. True transparent background. Detailed deliberate crisp 16-bit pixel clusters, warm outlines, earthy natural colors, same elevated 30 degree frontal view throughout, no labels, no text, no people, no map pins, no square terrain tiles. Top left: a broad low isolated flat-topped rocky hill with a grassy top and gently sloped green pampas base, inspired by Cerro de Palomas, not a sharp alpine peak. Top middle: a small grove of three native espinilho trees, low spreading sparse umbrella crowns, thorny gnarled trunks, dry savanna grass, a tiny yellow bird perched on a branch. Top right: modest border friendship plaza with a low pale stone obelisk, two tiny flag poles bearing simple Brazil and Uruguay flags, stone paving and two low benches, no sign or text. Bottom left: Cascata do Caracol inspired landscape, a single narrow white waterfall falling down a dark basalt cliff into a small blue-green pool, native trees at the top and sides, compact readable natural landmark silhouette. Bottom middle: old wooden waterwheel beside a low rough basalt stone wall, small wooden flume, sparse grass and flowers, evokes colonial craft of Caminhos de Pedra, no building or house. Bottom right: peaceful shaded small botanical memorial garden with a low mossy wall, three small rounded stone memorial markers, tiny carved cat silhouette and flowers, inspired by Edith Gaertner garden in Blumenau, no readable writing, no people, no spooky horror. Fully isolated organic contours and true alpha around each vignette; no rectangular backgrounds. Complete unclipped objects.
